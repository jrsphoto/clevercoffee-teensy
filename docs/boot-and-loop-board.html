The start-up check is thinner than it looks

Two tests: did the last run hang, and does the boiler probe read something believable. That is all.

Nothing checks the group probe, the flow meter, the scale, the knob, the link to the display, or whether the relay outputs actually work. A dead group probe boots completely clean.

Booted is not the same as ready to heat

The dry-fire interlock has no water probe to ask, so it refuses. Correct behaviour, not a boot failure, but they are two different states and the machine looks identical in both.

Only two of the ten move anything

Boiler temperature control and the sequence runner. Everything above them turns sensors into numbers; everything below carries out instructions. When something physically misbehaves, those two are where to look first.

The fault check sits near the bottom, after every handler has already run. It is a backstop, not the mechanism: each handler refuses on its own when a fault is latched.

04

What happens outside the loop

The loop does not switch the element. This is where the safety actually lives, and you cannot see it by reading the loop.

The element's own timer

every 10 ms · regardless of the loop
Has the loop allowed heating, and was that within the last 500 ms?
Yes → switch the element on for its share of the window
No, or too old → element OFF

The pump's speed request

expires after 1.5 s
Was a speed asked for again within 1.5 s?
Yes → keep running
No → stop

The watchdog

hardware · 4 s
Has the firmware checked in within 4 s?
Yes → carry on
No → RESET THE BOARD

Staying on requires constantly saying "still here"

If the firmware hangs, the heater does not stay on. Permission to heat goes stale within 500 ms and the timer switches the element off; the pump stops at 1.5 s; the watchdog resets the board at 4 s. The safe state is what you get by doing nothing.

The one stop with no sensor behind it is the shot's time limit. Weight and volume both rely on a sensor that can fail silently and never report the number being waited for. The clock cannot. Same reasoning for the flush cap, the backflush timings and the boiler fill's time cap.

05

The display, in one paragraph

The ESP32-S3 starts up on its own, brings up the screen, joins WiFi, serves the web pages and sends its stored settings down to the Teensy. After that it reads the Teensy's status and redraws. It switches nothing and holds no safety: a touch is a request, and the Teensy decides. If either board reboots, the two notice and re-sync by themselves.
